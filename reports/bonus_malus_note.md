**BonusMalus.** BonusMalus is the strongest rating factor in GLM-A: removing it raises learn deviance by 4,416 and CV deviance by 0.008125, 63% of GLM-A's whole CV gain over an intercept-only model (`glm_a_factor_importance.csv`). It is not an independent risk characteristic: it is the insurer's record of past claims, so it partly double-counts the claims the model is predicting and depends on the bonus-malus rules in force (endogeneity). In a GLM with the other factors, claim frequency is associated with BonusMalus at a power of 2.07 (95% CI 2.01 to 2.14), against 1 if it moved one-for-one with the statutory coefficient. This is an association, not evidence that the scale is mispriced: BonusMalus is entangled with driving experience, because young drivers start high and move down with experience as well as claims. Imposing the scale as an offset (no fitted BonusMalus terms) moves 27 of 61 other relativities by more than 5%, most of all young-driver relativities, which BonusMalus otherwise partly absorbs (`bm_crm_offset_relativities.csv`). Under the statutory scale (ASSUMPTION: Code des assurances art. A121-1, not verified here) the floor of 50 takes over a decade of claim-free driving, so young drivers should rarely be at 50. In the learn data 6.1% of exposure at ages 18-20 is at 50, against 25% at 30-34 and 80% at 45-54 (`bm_by_driver_age.csv`). For most young drivers BonusMalus therefore measures experience rather than claims, which is why the DrivAge x BonusMalus interaction exists; for the minority at 50 the recorded BonusMalus is probably not their own record (for example a transferred or main-driver coefficient), which the data cannot confirm. Results are not transferable to a market with a different no-claims system, such as UK NCD.
